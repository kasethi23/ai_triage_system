On-call call volume when a physician is on service

A metadata-only look at inbound and outbound telephone load during CCU and ward (board) service weeks, prepared to size the problem for the proposed call tracking and triage tool. No message content, no patient information.

During a CCU service week an EP attending fielded 285 calls in seven days, five times the non-service baseline, and missed roughly 4 in 10 incoming calls, most while already engaged in patient care or fielding an overlapping call. This is the gap the communication tool is meant to close.

A single phone cannot take two lines at once. In the CCU week nearly half of all calls stacked on top of another, and most unanswered calls occurred inside one of those clusters. The rest were deferred for a rational reason: while seeing a patient or at the bedside, breaking off a clinical conversation to take an untriaged call is disruptive, and with no way to judge urgency in advance, the call was tabled. This is the core argument for the tool. Automated intake that logs, timestamps, and ranks urgency turns a returned call from a guess into a decision.

A missed call rarely closed with a tidy callback. Returning a call needs a free moment the physician did not have, so only about 1 in 5 were returned the same day. Instead nearly half the callers simply rang again within the hour. That re-call churn is not separate from the volume problem, it is part of it: those repeat calls sit inside the 285 total and feed straight back into the collisions above. A logged and triaged queue absorbs both sides. Callers stop re-dialing because their request is captured and ranked, and the physician stops guessing who to call back first.

Calls are only half the channel. Text traffic ran near 110 messages a day on service, almost double the baseline of 60, split evenly between sent and received, which is the signature of two-way coordination rather than broadcast. Connected calls are measured directly at 12.2 hours for the CCU week. Adding texts at a conservative 45 seconds of attention each brings estimated communication time to roughly 22 hours across the week, about 3 hours every day on top of clinical work. The text portion is an estimate; the call time is measured.

What the data shows

  • VolumeService coverage multiplies telephone load. A ward week ran at 19 calls per day and a CCU week at 41, against a baseline near 8. The CCU week alone was 285 calls.
  • Missed callsOf 196 incoming calls in the CCU week, 78 went unanswered (40%). The ward week missed 24%. Calls were not declined casually. They arrived while the physician was seeing patients, at the bedside, already on another line, or could not be judged for urgency and so were deferred.
  • CCU lineThe CCU telephone accounted for 34 calls in the CCU week and 12 in the ward week. It is a primary channel, and it is invisible in text or paging records.
  • Time costConnected calls consumed 12.2 hours in the CCU week and 6.1 in the ward week, on top of direct patient care.
  • Text loadText messaging rose alongside calls, from 60 a day off service to about 110 a day on service, evenly sent and received. Unlike the truncated call history, the text record is complete throughout.
  • TimingLoad is concentrated in daytime hours, peaking mid-afternoon, with a lighter overnight tail (11 overnight calls in the CCU week, 20 in the ward week).

Scope and data provenance

Source. Telephone call metadata from a single physician device: direction, answered or missed status, duration, counterparty number, and timestamp. No audio, no voicemail, no message content, no patient identifiers. Service weeks were mapped from the 2026 physician assignment schedule.
  • Complete-data window is March 1 to July 6, 2026. Earlier call history was truncated by the device and is not reliable, so it is excluded. The text record is complete throughout.
  • This captures one CCU week and one ward week. The signal is strong and consistent, but wider sampling across more service weeks will firm up the averages, and re-running the same extraction as new weeks accrue will do exactly that.
  • Voice calls only for the call figures. Numeric paging is not captured and would add to the totals.
  • Call talk-time is measured. Text handling time is an estimate at roughly 45 seconds per message, and text counts include some personal traffic, so the combined communication-time figure is indicative rather than exact.
