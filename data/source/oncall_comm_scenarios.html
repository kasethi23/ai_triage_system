On-call communication: synthetic scenarios for triage design

Representative examples of the calls, voicemails, and texts an EP attending fields while on service, spanning roles, channels, and urgency. Built to show engineering what the tool must capture, transcribe, triage, and document.

Synthetic · no real patient data

Every message below is invented. None comes from real message data. The clinical patterns are authentic to electrophysiology so the examples read true to a working EP, but no patient, colleague, or event is real. Each card is tagged with the sender role, the channel, an urgency tier, and a triage note describing what a good tool should do with it.

Urgency tiers

Critical Interrupt care now. Real-time.
Urgent Respond within minutes. Active issue.
Routine Within hours. Clinically real, not timed.
FYI No callback. Closes a loop.

Channels

Text Two-way SMS or iMessage thread.
Voicemail One-way recorded message, transcribed.
Phone Live two-way call, transcribed.

CCU nursing

01CCU nurse to EP attendingTextUrgent
Weekday evening, EP on CCU service, seeing a consult on another unit.
CCU nurseHi Dr. Chan, bed 4 flipped into AF with RVR about 20 minutes ago. Rate 138 to 150, BP holding at 122/74, no chest pain. Rates not settling. Anything you would like?
Dr. ChanThanks. Is she anticoagulated from admission, and any signs of failure, raised JVP or crackles?
CCU nurseOn apixaban, last dose this morning. Lungs clear, JVP normal, sats 96 percent on room air.
Dr. ChanOK. Load amiodarone 150 over 10 minutes then 1 mg/min, recheck rate in 30. If systolic drops below 100 call me straight back. I will come by after this consult.
CCU nurseWill do, thank you.
Triage note   Stable but active arrhythmia. Needs a timely reply, not an interruption of a procedure. Tool should route to attention within minutes and capture the plan as a documented order trail.
02CCU nurse to EP attendingTextCritical
02:10, overnight on call, EP asleep at home.
CCU nurseDr. Chan, sorry to wake you. Your ICD patient in bed 2 has had 3 shocks in 15 minutes, awake and distressed, looks like recurrent VT on the monitor. What would you like us to do?
Dr. ChanThat is electrical storm. Put me on speaker at the bedside. Start IV amiodarone bolus, check and replace potassium and magnesium, and call the on-call cardiology fellow to help. Is he still perfusing between episodes?
CCU nurseYes, BP recovers to 110 between episodes, GCS 15.
Dr. ChanGood. Add a beta-blocker if the BP tolerates it, keep pads on him. I am coming in now.
Triage note   The call that must never be missed. This is the failure mode a missed-call system has to prevent. Tool should mark critical, break through do-not-disturb, and auto-escalate to a backup if unacknowledged in 60 seconds.

Outside and referring physicians

03Emergency physician, referring site, to EP attendingPhoneCritical
Mid-afternoon, EP at a bedside on the ward.
Call transcript

ED physician: Dr. Chan, it is the emergency department at the referring site. I have a patient in their sixties with a regular wide-complex tachycardia at 190, looks like VT, BP 84 systolic and borderline. Adenosine did nothing. Awake but clammy. I need EP input now. Do we shock, or do you want procainamide?

Dr. Chan: If he is borderline and symptomatic, do not wait on drugs. Sedate and perform a synchronized cardioversion, 150 biphasic. Get the pads on now. I am on my way down, keep this line open.

ED physician: Copy, charging now.

Triage note   Live decision under time pressure. Interrupt everything. Tool should support a held-open line and one-tap escalation, and log the verbal order for the record.
04Community cardiologist to EP attendingVoicemailRoutine
Left during a procedure. Heard two hours later.
Voicemail transcript

Hi William, calling from the community office. I have a patient in her fifties, very symptomatic paroxysmal AF, failed flecainide and sotalol, keen on ablation. Structurally normal heart, I think a good candidate. Wanted to see if you would take the referral and roughly what your wait looks like. Call me back when you get a chance, no rush. Thanks.

Triage note   A referral, not a page. Belongs in a queue with the caller number and reason captured, so it never competes with a clinical alert and never needs a live callback to find out it was routine.

Nurse practitioner, device clinic

05Nurse practitioner, device clinic, to EP attendingTextRoutine
Weekday morning, EP in clinic.
NPMorning Dr. Chan. Remote transmission on one of your pacemaker patients flagged 3 episodes of AT/AF overnight, longest 4 hours, rates well controlled. CHADS score is 3, currently not anticoagulated. Book a call to discuss a DOAC, or bring to next device round?
Dr. ChanCHADS 3 with 4 hours documented, yes let us anticoagulate. Send a note to the family doctor to start apixaban 5 twice daily and arrange follow up. Confirm no bleeding history first.
NPWill confirm no bleeding contraindication, then send. Thanks.
Triage note   Clinically important, not time-critical. Ideal for asynchronous, documented handling. Tool should let this be resolved without a phone call at all, and log the decision to the patient record.

Ward nursing

06Ward nurse to EP attendingTextUrgent
Afternoon, EP on ward service, in a family meeting.
Ward nurseHi Dr. Chan, the patient who had the CRT-D yesterday has swelling over the device pocket, firm and a bit tense, bruising spreading. No external bleeding. He is on aspirin and was bridged. Concerned about a pocket hematoma. Can you take a look?
Dr. ChanHold any further antiplatelet or anticoagulant and do not massage it. Mark the margins with a pen and photograph it. Keep the arm still. I will assess within the hour. Page me if it expands quickly or he becomes unwell.
Ward nurseUnderstood, holding meds and marking now.
Triage note   Potential complication needing timely assessment. Tool should deliver clear interim instructions, timestamp them, and hold a photo attachment against the record.
07Ward nurse to EP attendingVoicemailFYI
Left mid-morning. No response required.
Voicemail transcript

Hi Dr. Chan, this is the ward calling. Just letting you know your post-ablation patient from this morning is settled and comfortable. Small amount of oozing at the right groin site, we have applied a pressure dressing, distal pulses are fine, no hematoma. Vitals stable. No callback needed, just keeping you in the loop. Thanks.

Triage note   Pure status update. The system should recognize a no-callback FYI and keep it out of the active queue, so it never reads as urgent until answered. Every one of these that gets returned is wasted time.

Between cases, list running behind.
Call transcript

OR nurse: Dr. Chan, we are turning over room 3. Your next case, the AF ablation, is pre-op ready, but the PFA consumables have not arrived from stores and anesthesia wants to know if you want general or conscious sedation. The mapping rep is not here yet either.

Dr. Chan: General for this one. Chase the rep, we cannot start mapping without them. I will come speak to anesthesia in five minutes. Hold the patient in pre-op until the consumables land.

OR nurse: Thanks, I will let anesthesia know.

Triage note   Operational and time-sensitive to list flow, not a clinical emergency. A distinct category the tool should recognize, since these compete with clinical calls but need different handling.

The core problem: simultaneous arrival

Three messages, four minutes, one phone

This is the pattern the data showed: in the CCU week, nearly half of all calls landed within five minutes of another. Here is why that matters. Without triage metadata, these three look identical until answered, and answering them in arrival order means the critical one waits behind a status update.

15:02Urgent   CCU nurse, text: AF with RVR in bed 4, rates not settling.
15:03Critical   ED physician, call: borderline VT at 190, needs EP input now.
15:05FYI   Ward nurse, voicemail: post-ablation patient settled, no callback needed.
What the tool must do   Rank by urgency, not arrival. Surface the critical VT first, hold the FYI out of the queue, and let the urgent AF wait its turn with its context attached. The physician stops guessing, and no one has to re-dial to be heard.
