Using the triage tool responsibly

For the people who use it: the staff who leave on-call messages, and the physician who reads the queue. It ranks incoming calls by urgency so the most urgent is answered first — it is a decision aid, not a replacement for judgment, and not for real emergencies (use the emergency line for a code).

The one rule

Don't game the queue

The tool ranks on clinical facts — the patient's condition, vitals, what's changing, and what you need — not on tone or urgency words. It is built to ignore dramatic phrasing, so dressing up a routine call to jump the line doesn't reliably work.

And it hurts everyone: inflating urgency buries a real emergency behind you, and once the ranking is noise the physician stops trusting it — making the tool worse than none. Every override and identity access is logged; there's nothing to win. Say what's true, and let the facts set the priority.

If you're leaving a message

Nurse · referring physician · staff · family

DOState the situation plainly — who/where, what's happening, the vitals, and what you need (SBAR works best).
DOEnter the bed/room on the keypad when prompted — it's more accurate than saying it.
DOSay "no callback needed" when you're just closing the loop — it keeps the queue clean.
DON'TDon't inflate urgency to be seen sooner — the facts already rank it correctly.
DON'TDon't downplay a real emergency — if minutes matter, say so, or use the emergency bypass / line.
DON'TDon't leave a message with no clinical content ("call me about bed 7") — it can't be triaged and gets held for a human.

If you're the on-call physician

You are responsible for the call — the AI assists

DOTreat the label as a suggestion — read the transcript and use your judgment.
DOLook promptly at anything flagged for review (insufficient detail / low confidence) — those are the ones the AI is unsure about.
DOCorrect a wrong classification — it feeds back (after human review) to improve the tool. Correct thoughtfully.
DON'TDon't assume a "routine" label means safe — if it reads worse than its tag, act on your read.
DON'TDon't view or export identity you don't need — re-identification is audited (PHIPA). No screenshots or sharing.

Every user should know — this is a prototype

  • The AI has a small but real error rate (it will occasionally mis-rank) — which is exactly why the physician stays in the loop.
  • Not approved for real patient data yet. In testing/demos, use fabricated names and details only.
  • Audio passes through third-party services (phone provider, transcription) before de-identification — don't speak real patient identifiers into a demo call.
  • If the tool does something wrong, flag/override it and tell the team — don't quietly work around it.
